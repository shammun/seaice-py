## 1. Concepts (what the chapter teaches, in our words)

- Chapter 9 **derives nothing new**. It applies Ch. 3 (ice concentration) and Ch. 6/7 (floe identification) to
  **model-basin (ice-tank) imagery** from the DYPIC May-2011 HSVA campaign — a drillship in oblique tow, heading
  180°, towing speed stepped mid-run. **The chapter contains no numbered equations at all**; its formal content
  is **Algorithm 7** (p. 206), the three re-segmentation criteria of p. 205 and four tables.
- **§9.1** A 54 m level-ice sheet is cut into **one 1.50 m strip, four 1.00 m, nine 0.50 m**, each cross-cut into
  squares, and spread over 64 m of tank. Because every strip spans the same tank length, the **area share equals
  the width share**: 4.5 : 4.0 : 1.5 of 10.0 m = **45 / 40 / 15 %** against the size labels 0.50 / 1.00 / 1.50 m
  — exactly **Table 9.1** in the printed order (R8, §6). Runs 5200 (remove floes), 5300 (halve diagonally) and
  5400 (re-insert) vary IC and size mix.
- **§9.2.1** Three ice-concentration methods on the stitched overall tank image: **global Otsu** (Fig. 9.3),
  **2×3 block local Otsu** (Fig. 9.4) and **k-means k = 2** (Fig. 9.5). They agree because the histogram is
  bimodal (Fig. 9.2), illumination is near-uniform and there is one ice type — the book's own explanation of why
  Otsu's assumptions hold here. All three land 3–8 % **below** the target, blamed on imperfect sheet preparation
  and an out-of-tank bright triangle in the frame's upper right.
- The Fig. 9.4 caption's "average IC" is the **unweighted mean of the six block ICs**, not ch3's pixel-weighted
  `Σ num/(r·c)` — ch9's script deleted that line. On six *equal* blocks the two definitions are **algebraically
  identical**, so the arithmetic is consistent with the reading but cannot prove it (lesson 8).
- **§9.2.2** IC from video: four > 24-min 25 fps clips decimated to **1 fps**. Pre-processing (Fig. 9.6) crops
  the tank impediments away (`x 125:521`, `y 180:400` → 221×397) and **blanks the vessel** with a black
  rectangle (`x 268:380`, `y 307:400` → 94×113) — *before* the crop and *before* `rgb2gray`, so the threshold is
  computed on a histogram containing 10 622 forced-black pixels. The IC denominator excludes the box:
  221·397 − 94·113 = **77 115**.
- Physical finding: IC **saturates at ~80–89 %** near the vessel whatever the start condition — the **tank-wall
  effect**, floes compacted toward the basin end. Tank-bottom lights reflecting off water and ice are classified
  as ice, biasing IC slightly high.
- **§9.3.1** When aligned square floes touch, binarization leaves **no hole** between them, the
  distance-transform seeds merge and one snake pass cannot split them (Fig. 9.11). The remedy is to **re-run
  contour initialization and segmentation** — an outer loop (`timer`), which is what makes ch9's parameter set
  differ from ch6's.
- The three criteria a well-segmented floe must satisfy (p. 205): area **<** `Ra`; convexity (floe area ÷
  minimum-area bounding **polygon** area) **>** `Rc`; length/width ratio of the minimum-area bounding
  **rectangle** **<** `Rl`. The authors warn these are specific to rectangular floes.
- **Algorithm 7** adds a stop rule the shipped code never implements: stop when all floes pass, **or when the
  floe count after steps N and N+1 is equal**, or at a maximum iteration count. `GVF_distance.m` computes `num`
  and never compares it (gap G1).
- **§9.3.2** One GVF parameter set cannot serve the whole tank image → split into **20 overlapping sub-images**
  with a per-tile iteration count (**Table 9.4**), stitch, then run ch7's ice shape enhancement. **No `.m` drives
  this** (gap G2 in part). Light reflections can pull a deforming contour off the true boundary (Fig. 9.14).
- **§9.3.2.1** For simulators each floe is modelled as **its minimum-area bounding rectangle** (`rect.m` →
  `minboundrect`), rectangles whose L/W ratio falls outside a band are dropped, and each kept rectangle carries
  an **overlap flag list** exactly as in §8.2.1 — but computed with **`polybool`**, not `polyxpoly`.
  Rectangularization makes floes overlap, so Σ rectangle area ÷ image area **over**-counts (87.75 %) while the
  segmentation IC **under**-counts (76.96 %, boundary pixels become water), with Otsu (83.17 %) in between.
- **§9.3.3** The same 1 fps decimation, Algorithm 7 per frame, and the **maximum floe area per frame** plotted
  against time (Fig. 9.18) — a warning signal for a risk-management system when the largest approaching floe is
  too big. The shipped `movie_floe.m` reads an AVI that **already holds segmented frames** (its `im2bw` has no
  level ⇒ 0.5 ⇒ `> 127.5`), so the segmentation itself has no shipped code.

## 4. MATLAB→Python lessons learned (new pitfalls; each is a row in `function_map.md`)

1. **Byte-identical file names are not byte-identical files — diff before you inherit.** `ch9/block_threshold.m`
   is **not** `ch3/local_Otsu.m`: 5 diff hunks, and one of them is **numeric** — line 26 is `if temp(r1,c1) >= th`
   where ch3 writes `>`, so every pixel *equal* to the threshold is ice in ch9 and water in ch3. ch9 also drops
   `num` and the overall `IC = Σ num/(r·c)`. `knowledge/` had asserted the two were the same code since setup;
   `block_otsu` now takes `compare='gt'|'ge'` with `'gt'` (ch3's verified behaviour) as the default. The same
   audit corrected the reuse count: ch9 shares **14** byte-identical files with ch6/ch7, not 13 —
   `minboundrect.m` is the 14th — and a test checks all 14 md5s against **both** the ch6 and the ch7 copy.
2. **A comparison against a *growing* vector is not a scalar comparison (E4).** `movie_otsu.m:39` is
   `if I(k).cdata(i,j) >= t*255` where `t` is the whole 1×k vector of thresholds so far, so `if` means `all(...)`
   and the effective threshold is the **running maximum** `max_{j≤k} t(j)`. R2025a: `uint8(50) >= [25 51]` →
   `[1 0]`, `if` **not taken**; `>= [25 40]` → taken; `if <empty>` → not taken. Measured consequence: **17 of 24
   frames change** when the bug is switched off, max ΔIC **3.09 pp**. *Read every `if` whose right-hand side
   accumulates.*
3. **`n = n+1` on a vector increments every element (E5).** The body should have been `n(k) = n(k)+1`. Closed
   form: `n_final[j] = Σ_{m≥j} count_m` (a reverse cumulative sum) — MATLAB's own probe on counts (7, 6, 4) gives
   `n = [17 10 4]` while `ic = [7 6 4]`. **`IC` escapes E5** (it reads `n(k)` inside the same iteration) but
   **not E4**. A consequence worth naming: the written `otsu.avi` and the plotted `IC` legitimately disagree —
   different rule (`>=` vs `im2bw`'s strict `>`) *and* different threshold (running max vs per-frame).
4. **A shipped `.m` can contain unreachable validation (E8).** `rect.m:32` is
   `if (nargin<3) || isempty(metric)` inside a **two-argument** function, so the whole `elseif` validation is
   dead code: R2025a runs `''`, `[]`, `'x'`, `'ap'`, the number `5` and `"a"` and returns the **identical** struct
   each time. Port the dead branch *as dead*, and honour the `isempty` half — `rect(bw, '')` must default to
   `'a'`, not raise (review N-c).
5. **A library function can return a malformed shape on a degenerate input, and the caller may then error (D9).**
   `minboundrect`'s `case 1` does `rectx = repmat(x,1,5)` on a **1×1** column, which is a **1×5 row**, so
   `rect.m:56` `v = [rectx, recty]` is a **1×10** and MATLAB's own `model_ice_model.m:32` `v(2,1,1)` raises
   `MATLAB:badsubscript`. `case 2` evaluates `perimeter` on the **closed 3-element** ring, so `Perimeter` is a
   **2×1 vector**. `case 0` returns `0×0` empties where our port returns `NaN`. We kept our values (reproducing
   the shapes would make two ch06 assertions ambiguous) and **pinned MATLAB's shapes in a test**, so a silent
   "fix" fails. Probe degenerate inputs against MATLAB rather than assuming a clean return.
6. **A null assignment past the end of a growing vector raises — it does not delete (R13).** The analysis
   predicted `floe(k) = max([])` would delete element `k`. It does not: MATLAB raises, and **the identifier
   depends on the right-hand side's form** — the literal `x(k) = []` is parsed as a deletion
   (`MATLAB:subsdeldimmismatch`) while a function call returning `0×0`, i.e. the shipped line, is an assignment
   (`MATLAB:matrix:singleSubscriptNumelMismatch`). Delete-and-shift only happens for `k <= numel`, which the loop
   never reaches. `movie_floe(..., empty='raise')` is therefore the *literal* default.
7. **Video parity is a precondition, not a result.** Before any §9.2.2 / §9.3.3 claim, the container was proven
   to decode bit-identically in both engines: **0 differing bytes** on 22 118 400 and 9 216 000 samples, encode
   bit-identical to `VideoWriter('Uncompressed AVI')`, and the **axis order pinned against MATLAB's own `size()`**
   (`[480 640 3 24]` — `(H,W,3,N)`, not imageio's `(N,H,W,3)`). Everything downstream is an algorithm comparison
   *only because that is true*; on a real lossy DYPIC clip the labels would drop to `near`/`approx`.
8. **When two definitions coincide on your fixture, you have not discriminated them (review S1).** The first
   issue of the report claimed the N4 arithmetic *proves* the Fig. 9.4 caption is the unweighted block mean. For
   six **equal** blocks `Σ num/(r·c) = Σ(ic_b·rc/6)/(rc) = mean(ic_b)` **identically** — measured
   `0.8673501526073122` for both on the Tier-3 tank. The arithmetic is *consistent with* the reading and cannot
   discriminate it; the only real evidence is that ch9 **deleted** `local_Otsu.m:50`, which is evidence about the
   script, not about the caption. This is ch08's degenerate-fixture rule again: **an agreement your fixture
   forces is not evidence.** (Nit N-d: `num2str(83.146666…)` is `'83.1467'`, so the caption was hand-typed —
   "truncated" was a hypothesis, not a finding.)
9. **A known-answer assertion must be chosen so the *wrong* implementations fail it (review S9).** The first
   `ratio='minrect'` test asserted only that the ellipse and min-rect rules **disagree**. `29/9 = 3.2222` on an
   axis-aligned 30×10 block does not discriminate a bounding-box implementation, which gives the same number.
   The test now adds a **45°-rotated** lattice rectangle with half-extents 12, 3: min-rect **4.0** exactly,
   bounding box **1.0** — and both wrong rules (`bbox` and `short/long` = 9/29) are computed in the test and
   asserted to differ.
10. **`reimplemented` requires a reference or a known answer (review S5).** `tiled_segmentation` and
    `segment_video` were carefully written, documented and marked `# DEVIATION` — and are still **`unverified`**,
    because gap G2 has no shipped `.m` and the book supplies no grid shape, no overlap and no stitch rule, so
    neither half of the vocabulary's bar exists. Do not inflate a label for effort.
11. **A measurement must name its input (the ch07 rule, recurring twice here).** The `rect` / `model_ice_model`
    `exact` labels hold **on MATLAB's own `bw4`**. End-to-end from *our* `gvf_distance` the 28-px `bw1` residual
    propagates: `nS` 30 = 30 and `nM` 23 = 23, but `max|ΔArea| = 12.34 px²`, `ΣArea` 12 257.80 vs 12 243.54 and
    the overlap flags move **36 vs 34**, which moves `rect_ice_concentration` by 0.104 pp (82.1082 vs 82.0045 %).
    Both are true; only one is a parity number. All of it is now **ceiling-asserted** so a model-stage regression
    cannot hide behind the count-only `30`/`23` assertions.
12. **`np.hypot` is not `sqrt(dx²+dy²)`** (review S2). It is a different, more accurate algorithm and can differ
    in the last ulp — and `k = |v₁−v₂| / |v₃−v₂|` is then compared with **strict** `<`/`>` against 0.4/2.5 (E7).
    The two agree bit for bit on all 30 rectangles today; the literal form removes the class of doubt at zero
    cost. Same reasoning as the `single op double` rule: a discontinuous consumer amplifies 1 ulp.
13. **A patch record must be *generated*, not written by hand** (review S8). The hand-written `patches.json`
    printed the `movie_otsu` replacement block without the two lines `make_refs.py` actually appends (which a
    test then *consumes*) and recorded the removed range as 50–53 where line 50 is kept.
    `prepare_scratch` now writes the same generated record to both names and a test asserts all **38** recorded
    `removed` lines are byte-identical to the shipped `.m` line they claim to replace.
14. **The same erratum can have the opposite consequence in a different call (E9).** `if xx ~= NaN` is
    `if ~isempty(xx)` in both ch08 and ch09 — but ch08's `polyxpoly` reports only boundary **crossings**, so
    containment is invisible, while ch09's `polybool` returns the intersection **region**, so containment **is**
    detected (5 vertices, area 36). Copy the idiom, then ask what the wrapped routine actually returns.
    `polybool` also drops **partial-edge overlap** and **vertex touching** as empty while keeping a **1e-9-area**
    overlap — none of which the analysis had probed.
