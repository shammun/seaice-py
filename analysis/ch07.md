## 1. Sections and concepts

### 7.0 Chapter intro (p. 145)
- Ch6's GVF snake separates seemingly connected floes; **this chapter enhances the resulting shapes** so that
  individual floes and brash pieces can be *identified*, then classifies ice types and computes the FSD.
- Pipeline of the chapter: shape enhancement (§7.1) → general sea-ice processing (§7.2) → case studies (§7.3).
- "Since the output of the GVF snake segmentation method (Algorithm 1) is the segmented **binary** image, the ice
  shape enhancement algorithm will be a **binary operation**." (p. 145)
- **Fig. 7.1(a)** ice floe image with speckle; **Fig. 7.1(b)** its segmentation (holes + smaller pieces inside floes).
  Source: Zhang & Skjetne, *MIC* 35(4):293–301, 2014.

### 7.1 Ice shape enhancement (pp. 145–151)
- §7.1.1 **Morphological cleaning** = closing **then** opening [145]. Closing closes narrow cracks, fills long thin
  channels, eliminates holes smaller than the SE; opening breaks thin connections, removes small protrusions and
  regions that cannot contain the SE.
- **Fig. 7.2** — printed 13×23 binary matrix (a) with its closing (b), opening (c) and **cleaning (d) = opening of
  the closing**, using a **2×2 square** SE. *(Verified here: `core.morphology.imclose/imopen` with `ones((2,2))`
  reproduce (b), (c) and (d) **0 px**; sums 111 → 115 / 104 / 108.)*
- §7.1.2 **Connected-component extraction and labelling** by iterative constrained dilation, **Eq. (7.1)**.
  8-connectivity → 3×3 square SE (**Fig. 7.3**, completes at the 8th iteration); 4-connectivity → cross SE
  (**Fig. 7.4**, 7 blocks). "an arbitrary object pixel that is not yet assigned … is picked as the starting pixel p,
  and a unique label is assigned" [12].
- §7.1.3.1 **Iterative dilation hole filling**, **Eq. (7.2)** — same recursion on `A^c`; **Fig. 7.5** finishes at the
  7th iteration, final result = `X7 ∪ A`. Fails when a hole is 8-connected to the background (**Fig. 7.6**, cross SE
  finds only one of two 4-connected holes; **Fig. 7.7**, 3×3 square SE floods the whole background). Its weakness:
  it must be told whether the seed `p` is in a hole or in the background → "difficult to automatically fulfil".
- §7.1.3.2 **Reconstruction-based hole filling** (fully automatic) — border marker **Eq. (7.3)**, filled image
  **Eq. (7.4)**; **Fig. 7.8** (9×11 image, cross SE, converges at `D^(4)`). *(Verified here: `F^c`, the Eq. 7.3
  marker, `imreconstruct(Fm, Fc, conn=4)`, `H = R^c` and `H ∩ F^c` all reproduce the printed blocks 0 px, and
  `H == scipy.ndimage.binary_fill_holes(F)`.)*
- §7.1.4 **Ice shape enhancement algorithm** (**Algorithm 2**, p. 153): label the Algorithm-1 segmentation, sort
  the pieces **small → large**, clean + fill each in that order, superimpose into a fresh labelled image.
  "the arrangement of ice pieces in order of increasing size is required … otherwise, the smaller ice piece
  contained in a larger ice floe may not be removed." **Fig. 7.9** = enhancement of Fig. 7.1(b).

### 7.2 General sea ice image processing (pp. 151–162)
- §7.2.1 **Sea ice pixel extraction.** Otsu gives the *"light ice"* (Fig. 7.10(b)); k-means with **three or more**
  clusters gives all ice, the darkest cluster being water (Fig. 7.10(c)); *"dark ice"* = (c) − (b) (Fig. 7.10(d)).
  Rationale (p. 154–155): Otsu under-detects → **more "holes"**, which is what the §6.3.3 distance-transform contour
  initialisation needs; k-means alone leaves too few holes to initialise snakes in crowded areas; Otsu
  *multi*-thresholding for dark ice would cost more time. Cross-refers to Figs. 3.11(b)/(c) and 3.12(b).
- §7.2.2 **Sea ice edge detection** (**Algorithm 3**, p. 157): GVF from the grayscale; `ICE` by k-means; `LIGHT` by
  thresholding; `DARK = ICE − LIGHT`; Algorithm 1 on each; `SEGMENTATION = SEG_L + SEG_D` **labelled differently**
  ("light ice" = 1, "dark ice" = 0.5, water = 0) — otherwise connected light/dark pieces cannot be separated.
  **Fig. 7.11** = white (light) + gray (dark) segmentation.
- §7.2.3 **Sea ice shape enhancement** (**Algorithm 4**, p. 158): all detected pieces (light **and** dark) go into
  Algorithm 2 as **one** input — enhancing the layers independently would produce overlapping identifications. A
  **disk** SE whose radius follows **Eq. (7.5)**. **Fig. 7.12(a)–(c)** = crop of Fig. 7.10(a), its segmentation, its
  enhancement.
- Size colouring **Eq. (7.6)**; **Fig. 7.13** = labelled/colourised pieces, small = blue, large = red, piece
  positions (mean pixel position) as black dots. Printed colour-bar ticks: **3, 131, 277, 448, 656, 917, 1273**.
- §7.2.4 **Sea ice types classification and FSD** (**Algorithm 5**, p. 160): brash ice = "floating ice fragments no
  more than 2 m across" [113]; a tunable **brash-ice threshold** `T_floe` (pixel number, area or characteristic
  length) splits floes from brash; remaining ice pixels are **slush**; four layers = floe / brash / slush / water
  (**Fig. 7.14(a)–(d)**). "**a total of 154 ice floes and 189 brash ice pieces** are identified from Figure 7.10(a).
  The coverage percentages are **60.52 % ice floe, 3.34 % brash ice, 16.03 % slush, and 20.11 % water**."
  **Fig. 7.15** = FSD histogram (x 200…1400 by 200, y 5…45 by 5, colour-bar ticks 21, 114, 218, 333, 463, 612, 788).
  **Residue ice** = the detected edge pixels between connected floes (**Fig. 7.16**); counted as slush here but may
  be reported separately. Last paragraph p. 160: the ice-pixel extraction must be **updated after** enhancement —
  `PIXEL_seaice = IDENTIFICATION ∪ ICE` — because cleaning turns some water pixels into ice.

### 7.3 Case studies and discussion (pp. 163–174)
- §7.3.1 **Distorted overall sea ice image processing** on **Fig. 7.17** (large area, perspective distortion).
  - §7.3.1.1 **Local processing**: divide into smaller **overlapping** sub-regions (overlap to avoid the §6.5.3
    border effect), run Algorithm 3 per sub-region, drop the overlaps, stitch (**Fig. 7.18**).
  - §7.3.1.2 **Geometric calibration**: orthorectify per Appendix A.1.1; camera parameters unknown, so the authors
    **estimated shooting angle ≈ 20° and FOV = 46°** from the statistical similarity of near/far size
    distributions. **Nearest-neighbour** interpolation is *required* because each pixel carries a categorical
    label (light ice / dark ice / water). Calibrating **before** identification blurs boundaries and costs GVF
    iterations; **after** Algorithms 4–5 small far-range floes are mis-called brash → calibrate **on the segmented
    image, after Algorithm 3 and before Algorithm 4**.
  - §7.3.1.3 **Result** (**Algorithm 6**, p. 168). **Fig. 7.19** (before orthorectification) colour-bar ticks
    **21, 203, 426, 713, 1118, 1809, 7264**; **Fig. 7.20** (after) **21, 203, 426, 714, 1119, 1812, 9210**.
    "**A total of 2511 ice floes and 2624 brash ice pieces** are identified from Figure 7.17. The coverage
    percentages are **65.98 % ice floe, 5.03 % brash ice, 17.52 % slush, and 11.47 % water**." **Fig. 7.21** FSD
    (x 1000…10000 by 1000, y 100…800 by 100; ticks 201, 334, 488, 671, 894, 1181, 1586, 2277, 7824).
- §7.3.2 **A preliminary sensitivity study** on a **394 × 1038** MIZ image (**Fig. 7.22**) — *identified here as
  `sea_ice_test.jpg` displayed **transposed**, NCC **0.9787** against the (inverted) PDF bitmap; Fig. 7.23 0.8886,
  Fig. 7.25 0.7311 — see §5*. Initial contours on the "light ice" = **Fig. 7.23**.
  - §7.3.2.1 **Snake iterations** varied **1 → 122** with GVF fixed at **500** iterations (**Fig. 7.24**, y 200…1600,
    x ticks 1, 6, 11, …, 121). At 1 iteration the image is heavily over-segmented by the initial contours
    (**Fig. 7.25**) and Algorithm 4 then merges them into under-segmentation (**Fig. 7.26(a)**); the curves rise,
    peak, then fall to a steady value once every snake converges.
  - §7.3.2.2 **GVF iterations** varied **1 → 1500 in steps of 20** with the snake limit at **100** (**Fig. 7.27**,
    y 200…2000, x ticks 1, 61, 121, …, 1441). Too few GVF iterations ⇒ small capture range, noise dominates ⇒
    over-segmentation (**Fig. 7.28(a)**), settling as the field diffuses.
  - Brash boundaries are weaker than floe boundaries → the brash curves in Figs. 7.24 and 7.27 oscillate more.
  - Closing remark: the GVF iteration number should be **proportional to the image (object) size** [61], so tuned
    parameters transfer to images of similar object scale.

## 7. Risks & open questions

1. **R1 — `ice_shape_enhancement.m` cannot run unpatched in R2025a.** Lines 214–216
   `ch = get(h,'Children'); fvd = get(ch,'Faces'); fvcd = get(ch,'FaceVertexCData')` are HG1-only. Without a patch
   there is **no L2 reference for the chapter's central file**. Mitigation: comment out the figure blocks
   (209–237, and 182–206 if needed), change no literal, record the Deviation. Same issue in the deferred
   `color_hist*.m`.
2. **R2 — `imfill(b,'hole')` is called on a `double`, not a logical.** MATLAB therefore takes the *grayscale*
   branch and returns a **double**. `binary_fill_holes` would return a bool and silently change the class that
   `bwlabel`, `imclose` and `out(pp) = t` see downstream. Port `imfill.m` line by line (see §4).
3. **R3 — the book's `≥` vs the code's `>`.** Algorithm 5 (p. 160) says "sizes **equal to or larger than** `T_floe`";
   the code writes `if area0 > min_floe` and `elseif area0 > min_brash`, so with `min_brash = 1` every 1-pixel
   piece is **dropped from all three ice layers** (it still counts as `bk` ice → slush). Eq. (7.5) says
   `size ≥ size_th → r2` and the code writes `if r < se_th, r = 1; else r = 2` — **the code agrees with the
   equation**, but the shipped `README.docx` states "radius = 1, area **≤** se_th" — the README is the odd one out.
   Port the script literally and expose `book_threshold=True` for the `≥` form.
4. **R4 — ch7's `sea_ice_test.jpg` ≠ ch6's.** Same photo, different JPEG encoding (84 % of samples differ, max 43).
   Every ch06 number measured on it must be re-derived; pre-check says `bwlabel(bw,4)` gives **215** components
   here vs ch06's 231 (Otsu level is 162/255 on both). **Pre-check — to be confirmed by the verifier with the
   MATLAB run.** `core.io.load_image('ch07', 'sea_ice_test.jpg')` must resolve to `data/book/ch07/...`, never fall
   through to ch06's copy.
5. **R5 — book typo in Fig. 7.7(e) `X8`** (2 pixels, see §6). Decide explicitly: reproduce the *correct*
   iteration and annotate the figure comparison.
6. **R6 — `label2rgb` on Eq. 7.6 colour values.** `index` is not a 1..N label matrix; `max(index)` ≈ 10 000, so
   MATLAB builds a 10 000-entry `jet` LUT. Display-only, but the port must not blow up, and the report must not
   compare the colour PNGs (CUMULATIVE pitfall 25).
7. **R7 — MATLAB `hist` semantics.** `hist(x, n)` uses **centres**, `np.histogram` uses **edges**; with an explicit
   centre vector the outer bins are unbounded. Get this wrong and Fig. 7.15/7.21 and every FSD number shifts by
   half a bin.
8. **R8 — sort ties.** `[A, ind] = sort(ice_area)` with many equal areas (brash pieces of 2–5 px) determines the
   superimposition order and therefore `out`'s labels. MATLAB's `sort` is **stable**; use
   `np.argsort(kind='stable')` and pin it with a tie fixture.
9. **R9 — k-means non-determinism propagates.** `seg`/`bk` come from `seaice_kmean_gvf` (`approx` for labels).
   ch06 measured `bk` **0 px** on this image under `rng(0)`, so ch07's chain is reproducible in practice, but the
   report must repeat the caveat and compare **sorted centres + pixel agreement**, never labels.
10. **R10 — compute cost of §7.3.2.** Figs. 7.24/7.27 are 25 + 75 full pipeline runs on 1038×394. Default the
    script to the **7 settings the book prints**; put the full sweep behind `--full-sweep` and allow `--downscale`.
    The book prints **no counts** for these curves (only axis ranges), so the sweep is `unverified` by
    construction — reproduce the *shape* of the curves and say so.
11. **R11 — full-image scratch arrays.** `b = zeros(size(out)); b(p) = 1` per piece makes `ice_shape_enhancement`
    O(N_pieces × M·N). Cropping to the piece's bounding box (padded by the SE reach) is mathematically identical
    for `imopen` but **not automatically for `imclose`**, which pre-pads with 0 and would then see a *different*
    border. Prove the crop equivalence with a fixture where a piece touches the image border, or do not crop.
12. **R12 — the three §7.2/§7.3 source images are missing.** Figs. 7.1, 7.9, 7.10–7.21 and every number attached
    to them (154/189, 2511/2624, the eight percentages, five colour-bar tick lists) are `unverified`. Do not
    fabricate them from a substitute image.
13. **R13 — Algorithm 6 has no code.** The tiling/overlap sizes, the stitching rule and the "remove the overlapping
    parts" step are described only in prose (p. 163–164). Any port is `reimplemented`; expose `tile`, `overlap`
    and the merge rule as parameters and document that the book fixes none of them.
14. **R14 — orthorectification depends on ch10.** §7.3.1.2 defers to Appendix A.1.1 (shooting angle 20°, FOV 46°).
    ch07 should expose only the *nearest-neighbour resampling of a categorical image* and call the ch10 primitive
    when it lands; do not write a second rectifier.
15. **R15 — no interactive input in ch07 itself.** `roipoly` appears only in the deferred `sea_ice_model.m`, and
    only in its **non-interactive 3-argument** form (already `exact` via `core.polygon.roipoly`). No `ginput`, no
    `imcrop` anywhere in ch7.
16. **Open question** — Fig. 7.5's "finishes at the 7th iteration" vs the printed blocks that end at `X6` before
    the union. State the convention used; both readings give the same final image.
