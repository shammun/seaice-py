## 1. Sections and concepts

### 9.0 Chapter intro (p. 195)
- Model-scale (ice-basin) experiments precede full-scale analysis; ice is scaled by a freezing process + chemicals.
- Data come from the **DYPIC** project's May-2011 HSVA campaign: a drillship in **oblique towing**, heading 180°,
  towing speed stepped halfway (full-scale drift 0.25 kn → 0.50 kn).
- The chapter applies Ch. 3 (IC) and Ch. 6/7 (floe identification) to that data; nothing new is *derived* here.

### 9.1 Experimental setup and model sea ice image data (pp. 195–196)
- Two model vessels (Arctic drillship, polar research vessel); only the drillship in oblique tow is analysed.
- Managed ice made by cutting a **54 m** level-ice sheet into **squares** and spreading it over **64 m** of tank.
- Cutting: one 1.50 m strip, four 1.00 m strips, nine 0.50 m strips, then cross-cut to squares → the 45/40/15 %
  size mix of **Table 9.1**. Run 5200 removes floes (IC ↓), 5300 halves every floe diagonally, 5400 re-inserts them.
- Cameras: (i) a **top-view still camera**, 28 photos stitched into one overall tank image (→ §9.2.1, Fig. 9.1);
  (ii) a **top-view video camera on the carriage** (→ §9.2.2, §9.3.3); (iii) side/front and fish-eye cameras.
- **Table 9.1** target values: 5100 → 86 %, 0.50/1.00/1.50 m; 5200 → 70 %, same sizes; 5300 → 70 %,
  0.25/0.50/0.75 m; 5400 → 86 %, 0.25/0.50/0.75 m.

### 9.2 Ice concentration (p. 196)
- Otsu thresholding and *k*-means clustering from Ch. 3 are applied unchanged to the model-ice data.

#### 9.2.1 IC from the overall tank image (pp. 197–199)
- Fig. 9.1 = the stitched overall tank image of run 5100; Fig. 9.2 = its grayscale histogram (**clearly bimodal**,
  x 0–300, y 0–2.5×10⁵).
- Three methods: **global Otsu** (Fig. 9.3), **local (2×3 block) Otsu** (Fig. 9.4), **k-means, k = 2** (Fig. 9.5).
- Because the histogram is bimodal *and* the illumination is nearly uniform *and* there is only one ice type, the
  two Otsu assumptions hold, so global ≈ local ≈ k-means (the book's own explanation, p. 199).
- All three land **3–8 % below the target**; the authors blame imperfect sheet preparation plus the out-of-tank
  bright triangle in the image's upper-right corner (visible in the Fig. 9.1 bitmap).
- **Table 9.2** collects the six numbers (see §4).

#### 9.2.2 IC from model sea ice video (pp. 199–201)
- Four HSVA videos, > 24 min at 25 fps; **one frame per second is decimated** and fed to the program.
- Fish-eye distortion judged negligible here (a rectifier is in **Appendix A.2** = ch10).
- Tank-bottom **lights reflect** off water and ice → they can be classified as ice → IC biased slightly high.
- Pre-processing (Fig. 9.6): crop away the tank impediments, and **blank the vessel with a black rectangle**.
- Global Otsu and k-means are run per frame; IC(t) is plotted (Figs. 9.8, 9.9) and the Otsu threshold(t) too
  (Fig. 9.10, all four runs).
- Physical finding: IC saturates at ~80–89 % in the vessel's vicinity regardless of the start condition — the
  **tank wall effect** (floes compacted toward the basin end). Run 5100 saturates at ~89 % after ~200 s.

### 9.3 Ice floe identification (pp. 201–211)

#### 9.3.1 Contour initialization for crowded rectangular floes (pp. 204–205)
- When aligned square floes touch, binarization leaves **no "hole"** between them, so the distance-transform seeds
  merge and the snake cannot split them (Fig. 9.11(a)–(c)).
- Remedy: run contour initialization + segmentation **again** (Fig. 9.11(d)(e)) — i.e. an outer loop.
- Three **criteria a well-segmented floe must satisfy** (p. 205):
  1. area **less** than a threshold;
  2. **convexity** (floe area ÷ minimum-area bounding polygon area) **larger** than a threshold;
  3. the **length/width ratio of the minimum-area bounding rectangle less** than a threshold.
- Stop rule (text): all floes satisfy the criteria, **or** the total floe count after steps *N* and *N*+1 is equal,
  **or** an absolute maximum iteration count. → **Algorithm 7** (p. 206), the only numbered algorithm in the chapter.
- The authors warn that these criteria are specific to rectangles; for other shapes replace or drop them.

#### 9.3.2 Ice floe identification for the overall tank image (pp. 205–207)
- A single GVF parameter set cannot serve the whole tank image → split into **20 overlapping sub-images** (overlap
  to avoid border effects), run Algorithm 7 locally with a per-sub-image GVF iteration count (**Table 9.4**), then
  stitch, then run the Ch. 7 **ice shape enhancement** to fill noise holes.
- Results: Fig. 9.12 (colour-coded identification), Fig. 9.13 (floe-size-distribution histogram).
- Light reflections can pull a deforming contour off the true boundary (Fig. 9.14) — recommendation: switch the
  lights off or use a polarizer.

#### 9.3.2.1 Model sea ice floe modeling (pp. 207–208)
- For ice-structure simulators every floe is modelled as a **rectangle** = its **minimum-area bounding rectangle**.
- "Due to under- and over-segmentation, the rectangles with a length-to-width ratio **less than** a given threshold
  are removed" (p. 207) — see erratum **E7**: the code removes ratios outside `(k1, k2) = (0.4, 2.5)`, i.e. both
  extremes.
- Rectangularization makes some floes **overlap** (Fig. 9.15(b)); as in §8.2.1 the record per floe is
  **vertices, center, area, perimeter + an overlap flag list**.
- Fig. 9.16(a) = rectangle FSD; Fig. 9.16(b) = the per-bin error against Fig. 9.13.
- Three ICs are compared for the same scene (p. 208): **76.96 %** (Fig. 9.12, segmentation, boundary pixels turned
  to water), **83.17 %** (global Otsu), **87.75 %** (Σ rectangle areas ÷ image domain — higher because overlaps are
  counted twice, which "compensates" the boundary loss).

#### 9.3.3 Ice floe identification for video: maximum floe size (pp. 208–211)
- Same 1 fps decimation and impediment removal (Fig. 9.17(a)); Algorithm 7 per frame (Fig. 9.17(b)); the
  **maximum floe area per frame** is plotted against time (Fig. 9.18, 0–1000 s, 0–3×10⁴ px).
- Use case: warn a risk-management system when the largest floe approaching the protected vessel is too big.

### 5.2 `model_ice_model.m` vs ch08 `sea_ice_model.m` — precise diff
| aspect | `sea_ice_model.m` (ch6/ch7/ch8, 170 l) | `model_ice_model.m` (ch9, 72 l) |
|---|---|---|
| inputs | `ice_floe`, `brash_ice` (ch7 `IcePiece` with `PixelsPosition`), `img` | `S` (already-rectangularized, from `rect.m`), `img`, **`k1`, `k2`** |

| raster | `bw_floe` and `bw_brash`, both by `roipoly(double(img), …)` (l. 46/155) | one `bw`, by `roipoly(double(bw), v(:,1), v(:,2))` (l. 35) — `bw` is the same size as `img` |
| overlap test | **`polyxpoly`** for floe–floe, floe–brash, brash–floe; a pure circle test `d < r+r'` for brash–brash | **`polybool('intersection', …)`** for floe–floe only (l. 53) |
| overlap idiom | `if xx ~= NaN` (= `~isempty`) | `if xx ~= NaN` — **the same erratum, the opposite consequence** |
| output record | `struct('Vertices','Center','Area','Perimeter','Intersect')` where `Intersect` is a nested `struct('floe','brash')` | `struct('Vertices','Center','Area','Perimeter','**Intersection**')` — a flat index vector, different field name |
| figures | two (`bw_floe`, `bw_brash`) | one (`bw`) |

**The `~=NaN` consequence differs and it matters.** In ch08 the erratum made containment *undetectable* because
`polyxpoly` only reports boundary **crossings**. Here `polybool` returns the **intersection region**, so an
R2025a probe gives:

| case | `numel(xx)` | detected as overlap? |
|---|---|---|
| rectangle strictly **inside** another | **5** | **yes** (ch08's `polyxpoly` version would say no) |
| **disjoint** rectangles | 0 (`isempty`) | no ✔ |
| **partially overlapping** | 5 | yes ✔ |
| **edge-touching** (share one full edge, zero area) | **0 (`isempty`)** | **no** |

So `model_ice_model.m`'s overlap flag is *semantically correct* despite the idiom — the only rule a Python port
must copy exactly is the **edge-touching → no overlap** case, which naive Sutherland–Hodgman gets wrong (it returns
a degenerate zero-area polygon). `polybool` also emits a
`Warning: (X1,Y1) contains no external contours … counterclockwise-ordered vertices` for `minboundrect`'s ring
orientation and still computes the right answer.

**Verdict: ch08's `sea_ice_model` cannot be parameterised into this.** Disabling the hull, `polygeom`, the brash
branch and swapping `polyxpoly` → `polybool` would leave nothing of it. ch9 gets its own
`seaice.ch09_model_ice.model_ice_model`, and the shared pieces (`roipoly`, the `cat(1, …)` idiom, the `~=NaN`
flag, `icestruct` conventions) come from `core`.

### 5.3 The three movie scripts, frame by frame
Common pre-processing (identical in `movie_otsu.m` ll. 17–32 and `movie_kmeans.m` ll. 17–32):
```
mov(k).cdata = vidFrames(:,:,:,k);                  % k-th RGB frame, uint8
x1=125; x2=521; y1=180; y2=400;                     % keep window  -> 221 rows x 397 cols
x3=268; x4=380; y3=307;                             % vessel box   -> 94 rows x 113 cols
I1(k).cadata = frame(y1:y2, x1:x2, :);              % stored, NEVER USED again
I2(k).cadata = frame(y3:y2, x3:x4, :);              % stored, NEVER USED again
r2 = y2-y3+1 = 94;  c2 = x4-x3+1 = 113;
frame(y3:y2, x3:x4, :) = 0;                         % BLANK the vessel (Fig. 9.6(b))
frame = frame(y1:y2, x1:x2, :);                     % crop
```
Note the blanking happens **before** the crop and **before** `rgb2gray`, so the threshold/clusters are computed on a
histogram that contains 10 622 forced-black pixels (the book admits this on p. 200).

**`movie_otsu.m`** ll. 32–46 per frame `k`:
1. `I(k).cdata = rgb2gray(frame)` (221×397 uint8).
2. `t(k) = graythresh(I(k).cdata)` — `t` **grows into a 1×k vector**.
3. `n(k) = 0` — `n` grows too.
4. Double loop over all 87 737 pixels: **`if I(k).cdata(i,j) >= t*255`**.
   **Erratum E4**: `t` is the whole vector, so `scalar >= vector` is a *logical vector* and `if` means `all(...)`,
   i.e. the pixel must be ≥ **max(t(1:k))·255**, the running maximum of every threshold so far. Confirmed in
   R2025a (`uint8(50) >= [25 51]` → `[true false]`, `if` **not taken**).
   **Erratum E5**: the body is **`n = n+1`**, not `n(k) = n(k)+1`, so every element of `n` is incremented.
   R2025a probe over three frames with `t = [0.1 0.2 0.3]`: `n` ends `[6 3 1]` — `n(1)` was inflated from 3 to 6 by
   the later frames. `IC(k)` is read **inside** the same iteration, so `IC` itself is unharmed by E5; only the
   saved `n` vector is garbage. **E4 does harm `IC`.**
5. `bw(k).cadata = im2bw(I(k).cdata, t(k))` — the **per-frame** threshold with **strict `>`**. So the AVI that is
   written and the IC that is plotted **disagree** (different threshold rule *and* different threshold value).
6. **The exact plotted quantity** (l. 46, l. 51 `plot(k, IC(k))`):
   `IC(k) = #{(i,j) : gray_k(i,j) ≥ 255·max_{j≤k} t(j)} / (221·397 − 94·113)` = `… / 77 115`,
   x-axis = the **frame index** `k`, labelled "Time" because the video is decimated to 1 fps.
7. ll. 59–65 replay `bw(k).cadata` through `getframe` and `movie2avi(M,'otsu.avi','FPS',12)`.
   The commented `avifile` block (ll. 70–80) would have written **`Otsu.avi`** at `/256`.
   **Case inconsistency:** the live code writes `otsu.avi`, the dead code `Otsu.avi`. On Windows (and on MATLAB's
   own path resolution) they are the same file, so **this is not a bug today**; on a case-sensitive filesystem they
   would be two files, but since only one branch is live there is still no collision. Record it as a naming
   inconsistency, not a defect; the Python port writes `otsu.avi`.

**`movie_kmeans.m`** ll. 32–58 per frame `k`:
1. `rgb2gray` → `im2double` (→ [0,1]) → `double` → `si = size(...) = [221 397]` **before** flattening.
2. `ima(k).cdata = double(im(k).cdata(:))` — column-major vector, 87 737×1.
3. `map1 = kmeans(ima, 2, 'EmptyAction','singleton')` — Statistics Toolbox (**no** shadowing `kmeans.m` in ch9).
4. `s(i) = Σ(ima·[map==i]) / Σ[map==i]` for i = 1,2 — the cluster **mean intensity**. (`s` persists between frames
   but `kms = 2` is constant, so both entries are always overwritten — *no* stale-buffer bug here, unlike ch3.)
5. `a(k) = find(s == max(s))` — the **brighter** cluster = ice. (A two-way tie would make `find` return 2 elements
   and MATLAB would error on the scalar assignment; R2025a confirms `numel(find([3 3]==3)) == 2`.)
6. `p = find(map1 == a(k))`; `mask(p) = 1`.
7. **The exact plotted quantity**: `IC(k) = length(p) / (si(1)·si(2) − r2·c2)` = `#ice / 77 115`, the same
   denominator as Otsu. The blanked box is black, hence always in the dark cluster, so the numerator is unaffected.
8. `mask1 = uint8(mask·255)`; `out(k).cdata = reshape(mask1, 221, 397)`; then `movie2avi(M,'05400_kmeans.avi',12)`.
   **Erratum E6**: input is run **5100**, output is named run **5400**.

**Python video equivalent.** `VideoReader`+`read(v)` → a new `core.video.read_video(path) -> (H, W, 3, N) uint8`

plugin (installed, 0.6.0) and `cv2.VideoCapture` as a fallback (`cv2` 5.0.0 is built **with FFMPEG**).

codec and by ffmpeg can differ by several gray levels, which would move an Otsu threshold. Mitigation (§7): the
synthetic fixtures are written as **Uncompressed AVI** (MATLAB `VideoWriter(...,'Uncompressed AVI')`, Python
`codec='rawvideo'`), so both sides see byte-identical frames and the parity label can honestly be `exact`.

## 10. Risks and open questions

**R1 — No data for 3 of the 4 external inputs, and no Tier-2/Tier-4 path exists.** `04100_analyse.jpg`,
`dypic_05100_cam1_top.avi` and `05100.avi` are HSVA/DYPIC assets that were never published. Consequence: **every

§7.2/§7.3 images and ch08's §8.1 were. Synthetic fixtures give L2 parity against MATLAB but never L3.
*Mitigation*: P3–P5 generators + explicit `unverified` rows; never fabricate.

**R2 — Video decode parity.** MATLAB's `VideoReader` and ffmpeg can disagree on a lossy AVI, and a one-level
difference moves an Otsu threshold, which moves every IC. *Mitigation*: Uncompressed AVI fixtures and a dedicated
`ch09_video.mat` proving byte-identical decode **before** any algorithm claim. If a future reader supplies a real
(lossy) video, the label for that run must drop to `near`/`approx`.

**R3 — `movie2avi` and `mmreader` are gone from R2025a** (probed). Three of the seven ported files cannot run
unpatched. The patches are IO/graphics-only (`movie2avi` → `VideoWriter`+`writeVideo`; `mmreader` → `VideoReader`)
and must be recorded verbatim in `reference/ch09/patches.json` with proof that every quantity the removed lines
consumed (`M`, `bw(k).cadata`, `out(k).cdata`) is still computed and saved (ch08 precedent).

**R4 — `polybool` again (CUMULATIVE pitfall 49).** `model_ice_model.m` uses the compiled GPC front end. Its vertex
order is unreproducible, but here only **emptiness** is consumed, which is reproducible — **provided**
Sutherland–Hodgman's degenerate (zero-area, edge-touching) output is dropped. The R2025a probe pins the rule
(edge-touching → empty), but a **vertex-touching** pair and a pair sharing a *partial* edge were not probed; the
verifier must enumerate those. Residual risk: a floating-point near-touch where GPC and SH disagree.

**R5 — `erratum E4/E5` fidelity vs usefulness.** Reproducing `movie_otsu.m` literally makes IC(t) depend on the
*running maximum* threshold, which is not what Fig. 9.8/9.10 claims to show. Follow the project convention: the
literal behaviour is the default for the `.m` port (`running_max_bug=True`), the corrected per-frame form is a flag,
and every reported number states which one produced it (CUMULATIVE pitfall 61).

**R6 — Criterion 3 text↔code mismatch, inherited.** p. 205 specifies the **minimum-area bounding rectangle**
length/width ratio, `GVF_distance.m:90–95` computes the **ellipse** `MajorAxisLength/MinorAxisLength`. ch06 recorded

**R7 — Fig. 9.12 and Fig. 9.13 print different colour tick lists for data the text says is the same.**
Fig. 9.12 → 7 ticks, max **5878**; Figs. 9.13/9.16(a)/9.16(b) → 9 ticks, max **5952**. Either Fig. 9.12 was rendered

**R8 — Table 9.1 vs the strip arithmetic (N21).** "One strip of 1.50 m, four of 1.00 m and nine of 0.50 m coincide

**R9 — `rect.m`'s dead `metric` argument (E8) and `model_ice_model`'s asymmetric `k`.** Both are harmless in the

**R11 — Gap G2: §9.3.3's per-frame segmentation has no code.** `movie_floe.m` consumes an already-segmented AVI.
