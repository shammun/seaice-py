## 1. Chapter overview — sections and concepts

### 8.0 Chapter intro (pp. 175–176)
- Six numbered *applications* of the book's algorithms, all prose, no equations: (1) ice-management efficiency and
  hazardous-floe detection for Arctic drilling; (2) ice concentration + floe size as inputs to empirical ice-load
  formulas [76, 116] and floe-size-distribution modelling [15]; (3) initialising high-fidelity numerical models
  (Daley, Vachon, Sayed, Gürtner, Metrikin, Lubbad); (4) driving *synthetic* ice-field generators that draw polygons
  from the fitted distribution and pack them; (5) early warning of ice compaction ("slurry flow" → "pressured ice");
  (6) ice-drift velocity from sequential frames.
- Nothing here is implemented. Item (4) is what §8.2.1 + Appendix B exist for.

### 8.1 A shipborne camera system to acquire sea ice concentration at engineering scale (pp. 176–181)
- Context: expedition **OATRC'15** (NTNU + SPRS), icebreakers *Oden* and *Frej*; a 4-lens "Ice Concentration"
  camera covering ≈ 180° horizontally, **1 frame per 10 s**; only the middle two lenses could be calibrated (p. 176).
- **8.1.2 Methods** (pp. 176–179): the camera looks obliquely, so the raw image is perspective-distorted (more
  pixels near, fewer far). Full analytic orthorectification is **Appendix A.1.1** (→ our ch10); for real-time work
  the authors use the **linear rectification of Appendix A.1.2** [3, 57], which needs only the image coordinates of
  four corners of a rectangle of known physical size (a checkerboard) to build a transformation matrix. The
  checkerboard sits **5.90 m** above the sea plane and the camera **25.50 m**, so the `x'y'` plane must be projected
  a second time onto the sea plane. Ship motion is neglected; two white triangles are cropped away so that only
  near-field pixels are used; the analysed region is ≈ **400 m × 100 m** (p. 178).
- **Ice-pixel extraction**: *both* the **global Otsu threshold** (ch03 §3.1) and **k-means with k = 3** (ch03 §3.2)
  are applied to the grayscale rectified image. For Otsu, white = ice. For k-means the three clusters are read as
  water (black) / **wet ice** (gray: rubble, young ice, melt ponds) / dry ice (white), and **both the white and the
  gray cluster count as ice**. "If we choose two clusters for the k-means method (k = 2), then this method is
  approximately reduced to the Otsu thresholding method" (p. 179). Ice concentration = ice pixels / total pixels.
- **8.1.3 Results** (pp. 180–181): a 6-hour time series (Sep-26-2015 08:00–14:00, Fig. 8.5) compares the two methods
  against onboard ice-observer input. k-means is systematically **higher** (it keeps melt ponds/rubble as ice);
  Otsu under-estimates. Two named discrepancy events: **Event #1** dark young ice (k-means → ice, Otsu → water) and
  **Event #2** adverse illumination/shadow (Otsu → water, k-means less affected).
- **No MATLAB code and no data ship for §8.1.** Every figure is credited to Lu, Zhang, Lubbad, Løset & Skjetne, OTC
  Arctic Technology Conference 2016.

### 8.2 Numerical characterization of a real ice field for parametrization of an ice simulator (pp. 181–189)
- Context: NORUT remote-sensing mission, **78°55′N 11°56′E, Hamnerabben, Ny-Ålesund, 6–8 May 2011**; a **CryoWing
  UAV** (Table 8.1(a)) carrying a **Canon EOS 450D / EF 28 mm f/2.8**, **4290 × 2856** px (Table 8.1(b)).
- Figure 8.8 is an aerial MIZ image. "Since the image is undistorted, **Algorithms 3, 4 and 5 are carried out
  directly**" (p. 182) — i.e. the whole ch07 pipeline with no rectification and no tiling. Result: four layers
  (Fig. 8.9) at **76.73 % floe / 0.46 % brash / 9.05 % slush / 13.76 % water**, and **498 ice floes + 201 brash
  pieces**.
- Piece size = **number of pixels**; pieces are colour-coded by size with `*` (floe) / `·` (brash) at their centres
  (Fig. 8.10) — this is exactly ch07's Eq. (7.6) colouring, already ported. The floe-size histogram is Fig. 8.11.
- **8.2.1 Sea ice numerical modeling** (pp. 184–189): MIZ floes are "generally rounded" [129] but the segmented
  floes are not convex, so for numerical representation each **ice floe is replaced by its bounding
  minimum-area polygon** (in the code: the **convex hull**, `convhull(x, y, 'simplify', true)`), and each **brash
  piece by an area-equivalent disk** (`r = sqrt(A/π)`).
- "The polygonized floes will **not be smaller** than the actual identified floes, and they **may overlap** other
  floes and brash pieces" (p. 184). To support 3-D DEM simulators, an **"overlap flag"** is stored on every
  polygonized floe listing the serial numbers of the floes and brash pieces it intersects. (Note: this claim is
  about the *raster* footprint; the stored `Polygon.Area` — a continuous polygon area through pixel *centres* — is
  routinely **smaller** than the pixel count `Floe.Area`; see §9 L3-3.)
- Fig. 8.12 = polygonized floes (a) and circularized brash (b); Fig. 8.13 = close-up showing identified centres
  `*`/`·` vs modelled centres red `+`/`·`; Fig. 8.14 = polygonized floe-size histogram; Fig. 8.15 = the
  **difference** of the Fig. 8.11 and Fig. 8.14 histograms ("floe size distribution error … due to the shape
  simplification").
- "The processed results are stored as a MATLAB data structure … presented in **Appendix B**" (p. 184) — this is
  `SeaIce_Image_Structure.m`, and the book's Figs. B.1–B.3 (pp. 223–225) are screenshots of exactly that structure
  **derived from Figure 8.8**.
- Fig. 8.16 shows the field imported into a non-smooth **DEM** simulator [38, 96, 106] before (a) and after (b)
  collision-response overlap resolution [36]. **Out of scope** — no code, no data, third-party simulator.

### 8.3 Sea ice floe size statistic (pp. 189–194)
- Context: OATRC'15 again, but a **helicopter (AS-335NP off IB *Oden*)** with a 6-axis gyro-stabilised **Red Dragon**
  camera + Fujinon 25–300 mm lens (Fig. 8.17, Table 8.2): **5568 × 3132**, filming height ≈ **1314 m**, speed
  **40 m/s**, **25 fps**, sampling **0.1 Hz**, total coverage **3 × 3 nm**.
- Figure 8.18 is one frame. The pipeline identifies **2888 ice floes and 3452 brash pieces**, coverages
  **58.00 % floes / 4.85 % brash / 21.21 % slush / 15.94 % water** (p. 190).
- **Physical scale by reference object**: IB *Oden*'s known length is used to convert pixels to metres in frames
  taken at equivalent height. In the code this is the single literal `length_over_Pixel = 1.1794` **m/px**
  (`main_WL_new.m:13`); the book never prints it.
- **Eq. (8.1)** defines the size measure (MCD). Fig. 8.19 = floes coloured by MCD with white centre dots and an
  `[m]` colour bar saturating at `≥ 30`; Fig. 8.20 = the MCD histogram.
- **Eqs. (8.2)–(8.3)**: the *cumulative* floe size distribution and its power law. "The exponent α … is the slope of
  the power law curve on log-log plot (which is a straight line), and it is **estimated to be 1.3704** by fitting
  the power law distribution to the cumulative floe size distribution of Figure 8.19" (p. 192). Fig. 8.21 shows the
  fit, "showing a good fit for the smaller floe sizes".
- **Important discrepancy between text and code**: the code does **not** fit a straight line on the log-log plot.
  `fitting_iceFloes_distribution.m:42-44` runs `lsqcurvefit` — non-linear least squares on the **untransformed**
  `(L, N_c)` pairs. A log-log ordinary least-squares fit on the same data gives **α = 1.8697**, not 1.3704; the
  `lsqcurvefit` route gives **1.37036** (pre-check, §9 L3-4). The estimator is *not* MLE and there is **no
  goodness-of-fit statistic** anywhere in the chapter (no KS distance, no R², no AIC) — `resnorm`, `residual`,
  `exitflag`, `output`, `jacobian` are all requested from `lsqcurvefit` and then **discarded**.
- The chapter fits, in total, **three** distributions (only one of which the book plots):
  power law (used), **upper-truncated power law** and **Weibull** (both in
  `three_fitting_method_and_plotting.m`, whose call site `fitting_iceFloes_distribution.m:37` is **commented out**).

## 2. Concepts and equations

### 2.1 Every numbered equation in Chapter 8 (3 of them)

| Eq. | page | LaTeX (as printed) | what it computes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(8.1)** | 190 | $L_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{4A_i}{\pi}}$ | The "mean clipper [caliper] diameter" of floe *i* — in fact the **diameter of the circle with the same area** $A_i$. In `main_WL_new.m` $A_i$ is first converted to m²: `Poly_Area(i) = Polygon.Area * 1.1794^2` (l. 17) / `Raw_Area(i) = Floe(i).Area * 1.1794^2` (l. 30), then `sqrt(A*4/pi)` (l. 18 / 31). |
| **(8.2)** | 192 | $N_c(L) = \dfrac{N(\ge L)}{N_{total}}$ | Cumulative floe size distribution: the **fraction** of floes whose size is ≥ *L*. (The text calls it "the number of ice floes per unit area with size no smaller than L", but the formula is a plain fraction — no area normalisation. Code: `fitting_iceFloes_distribution.m:20-22`, `N_L(i) = size(find(MCD>=MCD(i)),2)/N_total`, evaluated at every observed $L_i$, so $N_c$ ranges from 1 down to $1/N_{total}$.) |
| **(8.3)** | 192 | $N_c(L) \propto L^{-\alpha}$ | Power-law model. In code the proportionality constant is fitted too: $\hat N_c(L)=\varepsilon_1 L^{-\varepsilon_2}$, $\alpha=\varepsilon_2$ (`F_powerlaw0 = @(epsilong,x)(epsilong(1)*(x.^(-1*epsilong(2))))`, l. 42). |
